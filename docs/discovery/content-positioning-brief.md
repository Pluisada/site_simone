## 4. INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

**The structure given for evaluation in this brief** (`Hero → Audience/problem recognition → Areas of Practice → About Simone → How It Works → Reviews → FAQ → CTA`) is **not accepted as-is.** It already improves on the earliest Discovery draft (it merges "About/positioning" and "Credentials" into one "About Simone" step), but it still carries three of the issues [[ux-strategy]] §9 identified and rejected in the prior structure, now confirmed by client-validation content:

1. **Hero + separate "Audience/problem recognition" section is redundant.** Every benchmark reviewed folds the audience/problem cue into the hero itself. Splitting them delays the visitor's first real content. **RECOMMENDATION (unchanged):** fold audience cues into the hero as short tags, not a full separate section.
2. **"Reviews" as a full section overstates current content.** **CLIENT VALIDATION (H1)** now confirms directly: no consented on-site testimonials exist; only third-party Google reviews under Simone's business. She explicitly wants attention drawn to those Google reviews and visitors redirected there — which *is* a lightweight link/badge pattern, not a full testimonial section. This resolves what was previously only an inference in [[ux-strategy]] §9.
3. **A single CTA at the very end reproduces the current site's diagnosed weakness** ([[ux-audit]] §10) and is inconsistent with **CLIENT VALIDATION (E2)**, which confirms WhatsApp as the sole channel — a single channel used sparingly is a bigger risk, not a smaller one, since there is no secondary path if a visitor loses the CTA. **RECOMMENDATION:** repeat the CTA at 3 points, not 1.
4. **Online consultation still has no explicit place** in the given structure, despite now having a fully specified process (**CLIENT VALIDATION D1, D2**). Leaving it implicit under "Areas of Practice" undersells a service that needs *more* explanation than in-person care, not less (benchmark evidence, TinyEYE — [[additional-benchmarks]] §6).

**RECOMMENDATION — final homepage sequence** (reaffirms and closes out [[ux-strategy]] §7, now with client-validated content behind every section):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 5. AREAS OF PRACTICE ARCHITECTURE

Six validated areas: atraso de linguagem, trocas na fala, distúrbios da fluência, motricidade orofacial, oratória, afasia/reabilitação de linguagem.

**Grouping options considered:**

**(a) By life stage (children vs. adults) — not recommended as the primary/only structure.**
Trade-off: several conditions plausibly span more than one life stage (e.g., distúrbios da fluência and afasia are not exclusively pediatric or exclusively adult), and no client-validation answer defines per-condition age boundaries beyond the general practice threshold (B1: 2y6m+). A hard age-based split risks either duplicating content or implying an age restriction that hasn't been confirmed per condition — a claim CLAUDE.md prohibits inventing.

**(b) By need/problem, in plain language — strong candidate, no longer blocked.**
Trade-off: this is the most visitor-friendly framing (matches §3's need statements directly) and is now supported by CLIENT VALIDATION for all six areas, including motricidade orofacial, whose plain-language description was resolved in the update above. This is now the strongest-evidenced grouping option of the four.

**(c) By service type: clinical/therapeutic vs. communication coaching — recommended as a secondary distinguishing layer.**
Trade-off: cleanly separates oratória (CLIENT VALIDATION B2: individual coaching, in-office and/or online) from the five clinical/therapeutic areas, which is exactly the differentiation [[ux-strategy]] §11 recommends. It does not, by itself, help a visitor scan *within* the five clinical areas.

**(d) Hybrid (RECOMMENDATION):**
- Primary structure: a flat, ordered list of scannable blocks — order driven by validated inquiry-priority signal from A2 (atraso de linguagem, trocas na fala, distúrbios da fluência, motricidade orofacial first, reflecting the parent-of-child/adolescent contact pattern; then oratória; then afasia). **Note (per the update above):** this ordering reflects A2's inquiry-volume evidence only — it is a scan-order decision, not a statement of relative importance. Afasia must receive the same block size, framing, and visual treatment as every other area; only its position in the list is informed by A2.
- Each block carries a **light audience tag** (e.g., "Crianças e adolescentes," "Adultos") rather than a hard navigational split — preserves scannability without the multi-door problem [[ux-strategy]] §9 rejected (Alternative 2).
- Oratória is **visually set apart in tone** (RECOMMENDATION, not yet a visual spec) as a coaching-style offering rather than a clinical condition — supported by its distinct delivery description in B2.
- Online-consultation eligibility (adults/adolescents only, per D1) is noted as a tag/note on the relevant blocks, not a separate section (§9 below).
- All six areas — including motricidade orofacial, now fully described (update above) — are ready for equal-quality content treatment; no area is blocked or under-specified relative to the others.

This hybrid keeps the section scannable, respects the audience-priority evidence in §2, treats afasia as equally weighted per the client's explicit instruction, and avoids fabricating age boundaries the client has not confirmed per condition.

## 8. HOW IT WORKS

Recommended structure, drawn directly from **CLIENT VALIDATION E1**:

1. **Primeiro contato (WhatsApp)** — an exchange of messages clarifying practical questions such as price and schedule.
2. **Consulta inicial / entrevista** — an interview where information about the complaint and the patient's history is collected.
3. **Avaliação** — begins from the information gathered in the interview.
4. **Processo terapêutico** — begins following the evaluation.

**Online path — RECOMMENDATION, drawn directly from CLIENT VALIDATION D2**, presented as the same 4-step process with these online-specific mechanics layered in where relevant:
- Stable connection, quiet/interruption-free environment required.
- Delivered via Google Meet.
- Access link sent approximately 10 minutes before the session.
- Punctual start.
- A registration form ("ficha de cadastro") and a commitment term ("termo de compromisso") are sent and must be completed/returned **before** the consultation.
- The client's own framing (D2: *"Funciona igual uma consulta presencial"*) should be preserved — the online path is a variant of the same process, not a lesser or separate one.

This is a structural recommendation, not final copy — exact step labels/wording are a copywriting task for a later phase.

## 15. CONTENT PRINCIPLES

- **Tone:** warm, direct, reassuring — especially for parent-facing content, where the underlying emotional state is often worry.
- **Vocabulary:** plain language first; clinical/technical terms may appear alongside, never instead of, a plain explanation (mirrors Simone's own style in C1).
- **Level of technical language:** low-to-moderate on the homepage; more clinical precision is acceptable in the credentials/Sobre content, where the audience may include referring professionals.
- **Trust:** built through specificity (real credentials, a real named process, real logistics) rather than superlative claims.
- **Warmth:** human and personal in address ("você," direct second person) even though no personal narrative is available (F2) — warmth here comes from clarity and directness, not from a life story.
- **Professionalism:** consistent with the formality of CFFa-registered specialist status; never casual or slang-driven.
- **Clarity:** one idea per block; short sentences; content organized to be scanned, not read as a wall of text (per [[ux-audit]] §7 and [[preliminary-design-direction]] §5).
- **Medical/clinical claim boundaries:** no cure, guarantee, "fastest," "100% effective," or comparative-superiority language; no outcome, timeframe, patient-count, or years-of-experience claims beyond what is validated; no answer to a FAQ question that isn't grounded in §12's validated sources.
